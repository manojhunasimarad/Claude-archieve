Yes, you can replicate research papers, and each one here lands at the stage where you have the tools for it. At your scale, replicating means rebuilding the method and reproducing the paper's trend on a small problem. It does not mean matching headline numbers that took the authors many GPUs.

  1. Read in three passes. First the abstract, headings and figures. Then the body, skipping proofs. Then re-derive it with the paper closed. This is Keshav's How to Read a Paper.
  2. Implement from the paper alone. Open reference code only to debug, after yours runs.
  3. Reproduce one result you can afford. The trend has to match. The absolute number usually will not.
  4. Change one thing. Predict the result before you run it.
  5. Write half a page. The claim, your setup, your number, and what differed.

If time is short, do the four marked Core. The federated learning papers at the end are the on-ramp to your own research: a replicated baseline is what a new result gets compared against.

Target: ML engineer for robotics, autonomy and edge AI. Your embedded and ROS 2 background is the part most ML candidates lack. The plan builds the ML side by hand and finishes where the two meet. The same preparation covers a general ML engineer role if you drop the ROS 2 and edge lists.

  1. Coding. Data structures and algorithms. Prepared by the DSA sheet tab.
  2. ML coding. Implement an algorithm from a blank file. Prepared by the ML coding tab and by Stages 2, 4 and 6.
  3. ML breadth. Short questions on fundamentals. Use the lists below, the MLStack.cafe question lists as a self-test, and chapter 3 of AIMLInterviews.
  4. ML system design. Chapter 4 of AIMLInterviews, then Designing Machine Learning Systems and AI Engineering on O'Reilly.
  5. Domain depth and your project. The reinforcement learning, ROS 2 and edge lists below, and the capstone.

Practice sites

  • deep-ml.comIn this tracker

    ML problems in Python, graded in the browser. The site says it has 1,200+ problems and that all of them are free. Its open set on GitHub has 164, which is what the ML coding tab and the offline runner use. It also has collections, projects such as building a GPT and an RL agent, and company interview tracks.

  • tensortonic.comLater

    The same idea with more systems work: implement-from-scratch problems, research-paper implementations and ML system design. The free plan lists 500+ problems. The reinforcement learning specialisation is in the Pro plan at $12 a month. Look at it at Stage 6 if you want more RL problems than the open set has.

  • mlstack.cafeQuestion bank

    2,103 interview questions by topic, for example Neural Networks 140, Statistics 59, LLMs 59, Reinforcement Learning 55 and Linear Regression 48. The question lists are open; the answers sit behind an Unlock button. Use the lists as a self-test: answer out loud, then check a textbook.
